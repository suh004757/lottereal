RENTAL BASICS

Renting in Korea: a quick guide

01

Wolse (monthly rent)

You pay a refundable deposit plus monthly rent. Building management fees and utilities are often separate, so compare the total monthly cost.

02

Jeonse (large refundable deposit)

You provide a much larger refundable deposit and usually pay little or no monthly rent. Ownership, prior rights and deposit-return risk require careful checking.

03

Banjeonse (higher deposit, lower monthly rent)

This market term describes a middle ground: a larger deposit than ordinary wolse in exchange for lower monthly rent.

BEFORE SIGNING

A practical rental checklist

  1. Compare the complete costConfirm the deposit, monthly rent, management fee, utilities, estimated brokerage fee and applicable statutory cap, and included furniture or appliances.
  2. Check the property and lessorVerify the landlord’s identity, ownership or authority to lease, and the property registry record before paying a deposit. Check existing rights and whether the proposed residential use is permitted.
  3. Record the important termsFor a short-term lease, state the exact move-out date and whether early termination is allowed. Also record repairs, furniture condition and deposit-return terms.
  4. Complete the applicable reportingComplete any required residence or address reporting, and check whether rental-contract reporting or a fixed-date procedure is needed for your contract.
Foreign resident address reporting and deposit protection

For a registered foreign resident, foreigner registration and a change-of-place-of-stay report replace ordinary resident registration and move-in reporting under Korean law. After taking possession of the home and completing the applicable registration or address report, the lease may become enforceable against third parties from the following day.

A fixed date on the written lease, together with the applicable opposability requirements, can establish priority repayment over later-ranking rights in an auction or public sale. It does not guarantee the return of the deposit, so ownership and prior claims still need to be checked before payment.

A registered foreign resident who changes address generally must report within 15 days of moving. Requirements can differ by immigration status, first-time foreigner registration and overseas-Korean residence reporting, so confirm the current procedure with the immigration office or local community service center.

A six-month term alone does not determine whether the Housing Lease Protection Act applies. However, a lease that is clearly for temporary use may fall outside the Act, so the applicable protection should be confirmed before signing.

Official Korean sources: Immigration Act Article 36, Article 88-2, and Housing Lease Protection Act Article 3-2 and Article 11.
